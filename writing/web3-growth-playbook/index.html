Web3 growth is community work with a token attached

What carries over from Web2 marketing, what doesn't, and the ten channels I'd still use to grow a Web3 project.

GrowthWeb3

First published on ByteMeData in January 2024 as "Web3 Growth Marketing 101". Revised and shortened in October 2026.

Web3 marketing has a reputation problem. Too much of it is hype, airdrops and promises of 100x. The projects that last tend to do something less glamorous: they build a community that understands the product and has a reason to stay.

What is actually different

Most of the Web2 toolkit still applies: search, content, email, partnerships. Three things change.

  • Users can own a stake. Tokens and on-chain assets turn some users into part-owners. That brings loyalty, and also scrutiny.
  • Everything is public. Wallet activity, token flows and treasury moves are on-chain. Your metrics can be checked by anyone, so inflated numbers get caught.
  • Trust starts low. After years of scams and collapses, most audiences arrive sceptical. Earning trust is the first job, not an afterthought.

Ten channels, ranked by how much I'd rely on them

1. Community

Discord, Telegram, forums and governance spaces are where Web3 users live. Treat members as early contributors, not an audience. Educate them, give them roles, and listen in public.

2. Educational content

Most of the market still doesn't understand how the technology works. Clear explainers, documentation and research build authority faster than announcements do.

3. Search

People still search before they buy, bridge or stake. Target the specific questions your users ask, not generic "best crypto" keywords.

4. Social, done live

X spaces, AMAs and live sessions work because people can ask hard questions and see you answer them.

5. Partnerships and integrations

In Web3, being integrated into other protocols is distribution. Each integration puts you in front of another community.

6. Credible voices

Work with people whose audience trusts their judgement, not people who will promote anything. One credible endorsement outweighs ten paid shills.

7. Email

A newsletter is the one channel no platform can take away. Use it for real updates, not hype.

8. Video

Short demos and explainers make complex products concrete.

9. Airdrops and incentives, carefully

Incentives bring people in fast. Badly designed, they mostly attract farmers who leave the day rewards end. Reward actions that signal real use, and measure who stays afterwards.

10. Experiments

The landscape changes every few months. Keep a small budget for trying new platforms and formats, and kill what doesn't work quickly.

The measure that matters

Follower counts and campaign signups are easy to inflate. Retention is not. The best growth question in Web3 is the same as anywhere else: of the people who came in, how many are still using the product a month later, and why?
